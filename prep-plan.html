Diagnosis and assumptions

ML system design is the binding constraint. It went badly at both Amazon and Oracle, and both coding rounds were cleared comfortably, so the loop is ending at the same place twice. LLM fundamentals are the secondary gap and overlap heavily with modern design rounds. Coding stays in the plan only as CodeSignal format drilling, which is a speed problem rather than a skill problem.

What this plan assumes

  • You are still working full-time at Samsung, so weekdays run 6 hours and weekends 10. If you move to leave or a notice period, the shape changes completely.
  • 45 days total: 30 at full intensity, then 15 at reduced hours. Days 7, 14, 21 and 28 are deliberately light rather than off.
  • Target roles are Senior MLE and Applied Scientist at MNCs and funded startups, with active or expected loops.
  • No new study websites, explainers or interactive artifacts get built during this month. Building teaches synthesis; loops test recall under observation. This month buys recall.

Hands-on artifact per component

The design artifact is the most important one in the month. It attacks the exact round that failed, and it gives you a second production-shaped system to talk about that is entirely yours.

ML system design: why it fails

Design rounds rarely fail on knowledge. They fail on five things, and it is worth identifying which one is yours before drilling, because the fixes are unrelated.

Your diagnosed mode

Different from the common one: you reach steps 5 and 6 but thin out once there. That is a content gap, not a pacing gap, so serving and monitoring matter more for you than design repetition does.

The structure, used identically every time

  1. Requirements — functional, then scale numbers, then the one metric that defines success (8 min)
  2. Data — sources, labels, how labels are actually obtained, leakage risks (7 min)
  3. Features and baselines — what the dumb baseline is, and why the model beats it (7 min)
  4. Model — choice, the alternative rejected, training cadence (8 min)
  5. Serving — latency budget, precompute vs online, caching, fallback when the model fails (8 min)
  6. Monitoring and iteration — drift, feedback loops, retraining triggers, A/B design (7 min)

The timings are the point. Most people fail by spending 25 minutes on steps 3 and 4 and never reaching 5 and 6, which is where senior signal lives.

Sixteen designs, delivered aloud and recorded

Two a day at weekends, one a day on weekdays. The last four matter most — both Amazon and Oracle are asking LLM-shaped design questions now, and those are also where ts and nlq give you real material to draw on rather than textbook answers.

Serving and monitoring

This is where both loops thinned out, and it is the highest-return block in the month. It is also the part of ML that reads as senior, because it is what you only learn by running something in production. You have run something in production, so the material exists — it has not been organised into interview answers.

Week 2 — serving and monitoring

Goal. Close the content gap that ended both loops. This is the most important week in the plan. Every design this week is scored on steps 5 and 6 alone — if serving and monitoring took under 15 minutes of the 45, the design failed regardless of how good the first half was.

Hands-on. Instrument the replica. A feature path shared between training and serving, p50 and p99 measured rather than guessed, prediction-distribution monitoring, a PSI or KL drift check per feature with a threshold you chose and can defend, and a fallback that returns a cached or heuristic prediction when the model times out. Days 31–37 — performance conditions

Goal. Everything happens as though observed. No untimed work, no restarting a design halfway, no pausing a recording.

Days 38–45 — company shaping, live loops, taper

Goal. This is where company-specific work finally belongs — one thin layer on a finished foundation, not a rebuilt curriculum per company.

Hands-on. Days 44–45 are three hours a day: one timed set, one design aloud, one story block. Then hold at that level for as long as the loops run — three hours a day indefinitely is sustainable, and it keeps you sharp without the fatigue that made weeks 1–4 costly.

Behavioral story bank

Twelve stories, written once and reused everywhere. Build three per week. Each runs under three minutes spoken, and each names a decision you made rather than work you contributed to. Tick a story once it has been delivered cold to camera under three minutes — not once it is written.

Keep the situation to two sentences and spend the time on the decision. Use “I” for decisions and “we” for execution, not the reverse. End every story with the result and one sentence on what you would do differently, because senior interviewers are listening for self-assessment.

If you only perfect three

Stories 2, 4 and 12 are the ones that separate senior from mid-level, and they are the ones most people have weakest.

Failure modes

Building instead of practising

Your strongest habit is producing excellent study material, and it will reassert itself around day 10 as an urge to make a tracker, a site, or a structured curriculum. That urge is the enemy of this plan. If you feel it, do a timed set instead — the tracker already exists, on this page.

Collapse, not decay

Intensity plans fail all at once. The defence is the hard rule: block A happens even on the worst day, even at 20 minutes. A month of 20-minute days beats nine good days and silence.

Practising what is comfortable

ML depth feels productive because you are good at it. Design and serving feel bad because they are where you are weak. The block that feels worst is the one moving the number.

If the schedule collapses, cut in this order

  1. ML depth reading
  2. Weekend weak-spot work
  3. Behavioral writing — but never behavioral rehearsal
  4. Design frequency, from four a week to two

Never cut the daily design block, the weekend simulation, or the weekly human mock.

Alongside a full-time job, 6 hours a day for 30 days with no rest day is above what most people sustain. The collapse ordering above exists because the realistic outcome is 4 to 5 hours on most days. That is still an enormous amount of preparation. Judge the month by whether the four numbers moved, not by whether every hour was served.
